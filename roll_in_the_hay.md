A Roll In the Hay

---

**ACT I, SCENE I. A street in Restenford.*
*Enter JACQUES DEVERRE, glowering after QUALTON's retreating form; KOTOS LONGFINGERS stands by.*

**JACQUES:**  
He turns his back upon my flung-down glove  
And spurns the honest challenge that I gave!  
What craven law excuses such a slight?  
Kotos, thou art well-read in books and men —  
Who best doth know the law of this our land?  
Beyond the Library, and Qualton's self,  
What scholar might unfold these tangled rules?

**KOTOS:**  
The Library were certain the first stop, *[gestures to his shelves]*  
Yet I have known some other doctors too.  
What is it that my lord would wish to learn?

**JACQUES:**  
By what strange law may Qualton spurn a duel,  
And what befalls him for that cowardice?

**KOTOS:**  
Ah, there thou treadst where Queen's Law and the rest  
Do part their ways upon a crooked road.  
'Tis like the Baron's word thou seek'st to know,  
For what he calls the law, the law becomes.

**JACQUES:**  
Nay, tell me more — Rhidalia's law alone  
Cannot be all. What of Esoteran rule,  
Or that hard code the frost-blood giants keep?  
Which of the three doth carry authority  
When one man's oath doth cross another's land?

*A soft light gathers, and ALEOL, a whisper in the dark of the street, a small map curled tight beneath one arm.*

**ALEOL:**
Here is thy chart, drawn true, of hall and stair,  
Of trap, of watch, and every guarded door.  
I searched it well, and found there naught of worth —  
Save one poor ring, not gold enough to keep.  

*She sets the map down, and is gone as she came — yet something of her lingers on the air: a cool breath, sudden, of autumn leaves, though summer holds the town entire.*  

**JACQUES:** *[breathing deep, distracted]*  
Kotos — dost thou not smell it? Autumn's breath,  
Here, now, in this the fullest heat of noon!  
It quickens something strange within my breast,  
As if my heart recalled a thing long lost.  
Tell me — dost thou remember how fall smells?  

**KOTOS:**
My lord, it has been but a long hot summer —  
No frost, no falling leaf, this many a month.  
*[a beat, then, turning the talk aside]*  
But thou didst ask of law, and law we'll have:  
Rhidalian law binds where Rhidalia's writ  
Yet reaches; the Esoteran custom holds  
Where merchants strike their bargains, treaty-bound;  
And Giantish law knows only kept or broken —  
No mercy lives between the two for them.  
The Yassa knows still less of mercy's shape:  
Join, or be broken — no third road is writ  
In all the Altan Ordu's iron code.  
Here, in this town, 'tis Rhidalia's law that rules,  
Yet ever colored by the Baron's whim.

**JACQUES:**  
Aye — and the Baroness, and her fair child.  
I trust they bear this trial as they may,  
Yet still I ache for gentle Andrella.  
Would I had aided her when last we met!  
No matter — how may we now lend our aid?  
And wherefore, Kotos, dost thou loose thy sword?

*KOTOS unbuckles his sword and belt, laying them gently upon the ground. Footsteps approach. Enter CAPTAIN GELPHIS with armed guardsmen and two great mastiffs, his face writ with c
are.*

**JACQUES:**  
Well met, good Gelphis! Glad I see a friend.  
How may we serve thee in thy hour of need?

*GELPHIS's brow eases, though his eyes hold fast their worry. In his haste, a word slips out unmeant.*

**GELPHIS:**  
The Baron —  
*[He catches himself, and says no more, his face gone pale as bone.]*

**JACQUES:**  
The Baron? Speak, man — what befalls the Baron?

**GELPHIS:**  
I have said too much already. Come with me —  
Thou must come with me straightway to the Baroness.

**JACQUES:**  
Excellent! Then lead us to her now with haste.

---

**SCENE II. The road to the castle.**

*GUY NORMAND bends to greet two young hounds, pups of Muff and Gruff, who hail him solemnly, then with eager joy, yet never once forget their murderous trade. JACQUES moves into the guardsmen's midst.*

**JACQUES:**  
Might we, good Gelphis, speak a word or two  
Upon our way?

**GELPHIS:**  
There's little trust just now  
Within the castle walls, Lord DeVerre. Speak on.

**JACQUES:**  
I hold this Qualton traitor to our realm.  
I heard him speak but now at the West Wind,  
And judge him peril to us, one and all.  
I've thrown my challenge down — how should I proceed?

**GELPHIS:**  
Qualton comes too, and stands, they say, with thee.  
For law, I'd have thee ask the Baroness.

*JACQUES nods, then eyes KOTOS with suspicion. KOTOS winks.*

---

**SCENE III. The throne room.**

*They pass through postern, portcullis, and guard, and come at last unto the throne room, wherein stands a coffin, and beside it the BARONESS clothed in widow's black. JACQUES staggers, clutching his head; NORMAND steadies him and brings him, wailing, before her.*

**JACQUES:**  
What madness, lady, hath befallen here?  
What wouldst thou have thy servants do this hour?

**BARONESS:**  
My lord was strangled by an assassin's knot,   
And by the Baron was there left behind,  
Three tokens, mute and strange, as if to mock us:  
A golden string, torn from a wandering bard's  
Own lute, who passed but lately out from Garrot;  
A ruby, prised from an old Stormcrow's throat,  
Who wore a raven wrought of ruby stone,  
He too of Garrot; and a button, red,  
From Innkeeper Abraham, of Garrot born.

**KOTOS:** *[aside]*  
Oh, subtlety! Oh, most exquisite art!  
Three baubles, and each one from Garrot's soil —  
Whoever laid this table wants us there.

**BARONESS:**  
Thou'lt swear an oath to me, here, now, this hour,  
And I shall know the truth that lies within.  
*[She lays her hand upon the coffin, and looks up at him.]*  
Didst thou then slay my husband?

**JACQUES:**  
No, my lady.  
I did not, nor did wish him dead — indeed,  
I longed to see him live a hundred years,  
And dreamed we two might yet be friends full long.

**BARONESS:** *[to QUALTON]*  
Didst thou then slay my husband?

**QUALTON:**  
No. I dined  
That night among these gentlemen — a feast  
Most excellent it was — I slew him not.

**TOLMAR:**  
Nor I. I had no hand in this dark deed.

**KOTOS:**  
Nor I — I did not kill him, nor did set  
Another's hand upon him; nor did know  
Of any plot to see him dead; nor bore  
Within my secret heart the wish for it.

*NORMAND swears the selfsame oath. KOTOS, ever wary, presses on —*

**KOTOS:**  
And wilt thou, Qualton, Tolmar, swear it full,  
Down to the smallest word, before us all?

*But the question passes by unanswered, brushed aside like dust upon the air.*

**BARONESS:**  
I have consulted with the days to come:  
The selfsame hand that slew my lord shall slay  
Myself within the turning of one week,  
And in a week thereafter, slay my child.  
Then, last of all, they mean to murder Qualton.

**JACQUES:**  
How fares your daughter now, my lady?

**BARONESS:**  
Vanished.  
We work now with the Tallis to reclaim her  
From out of Pelltar's tower — for its contents  
Do fall to their dominion and their charge.

**JACQUES:**  
Then straight to Tolmar we shall bend our steps.  
I swear to thee we'll spend our every power  
To bring thy daughter safe home to thine arms.

---

**SCENE IV. Before Tolmar.**

*KOTOS and NORMAND, having gone before with a plan already laid, find TOLMAR of one mind with them.*

**TOLMAR:**  
Here is a scroll, Lord DeVerre, for thy use.  
The tower and its contents are the Tallis's.

**NORMAND:**  
If we should win her free, we'll claim the tower.

**TOLMAR:**  
Its contents fall unto the Baroness's wizard.

**KOTOS:** *[smiling]*  
I sense a "but" that follows on this gift.

**TOLMAR:**  
The cracked and broken crystal — that alone  
Must be delivered up into our hands.

*KOTOS turns to DEVERRE and NORMAND.*

**KOTOS:**  
One word with Velkar first, and I am done.  
Are we then ready?

*He hastens to the old warrior, writing even as he speaks. Meanwhile, JACQUES regards QUALTON closely.*

**JACQUES:**  
Qualton, dost know why this town's named Restenford?  
The Rest runs strong, and a fair bridge doth cross it,  
Yet nowhere in this town there stands a ford.

**QUALTON:**  
There was a ford here once, DeVerre — three hundred  
Years gone, perhaps too long for thee to mind.

**JACQUES:**  
Nay — now I do recall it, now thou speak'st.  
My brother and I spoke more oft of this  
Fair place than ever once we stood upon it.  
There was a ford at Restenford, in truth.

**QUALTON:**  
I have no time for this — I go now to Andrella.

*QUALTON opens a book, takes on the shape of Guy Normand, and steps through into Pelltar's tower. Ere the others follow, each dons a guise not his own — for in that tower, roam things impossible, and magic bends to stranger law than theirs.*

---

**SCENE V. Within Pelltar's tower.**

*KOTOS returns, and together they enter — carpet plush and clean, floors arrayed with sleeper relics, whole skeletons in cases, stone made out of bone. Four helmed horrors, set at the corners of the room, stir to slay the trespassers.*

**JACQUES:**  
The stairs!

*TOLMAR leaps first up the left stairwell, flinging a curse to slow the moving armors, staggering one back.*

**KOTOS:**  
Call down thy guardian spirits, Qualton, now!

**QUALTON:** *[grumbling]*  
Must I be bullied every time to it?

*He summons, grudging, spirits of sharp thorn to ring them round. KOTOS draws the dagger of darkness and climbs, the others gathering close about him.*

**KOTOS:**  
Do the thing, Qualton!

**QUALTON:**  
I cannot see!  
Turn off this cursed dark!

*He stumbles blind past KOTOS in the murk. LORD OF GLASS appears behind a Horror in the shadow and hews it with his dwarven battleaxe — chips fly, yet the armor slips clear of most his blows.*

**LORD OF GLASS:**  
They see full well!

*KOTOS sheathes the dagger, leaps up into the study, and looses a scroll of fire upon the horrors — yet these things are dull and stubborn, deaf to fear, and scarcely feel the flame.*

**TOLMAR:**  
I have it!

*He runs to the door atop the stair and, from an abyssal tome, begins a spell to seal it fast. NORMAND, QUALTON, and DEVERRE climb into the library, ringed about by Qualton's stabbing shadow-guardians.*

---

**SCENE VI. The library, and the cards.**

*KOTOS snatches the weightiest book in sight, a second dark tome tucked beside the first — though nowhere in the tower is there sign of Peltar's self — and races up the farther stair. As he nears the door, ANDRELLA appears upon the threshold, sounds a horn of ancient make that summons seven temple guardians of the Tzalite dead, and slams the door behind her.*

**KOTOS:**  
Glad thou hast sealed that door — rough men without.

*He tears a rift within the very air, and steps clean through the door, and through the guards, to stand now at her side; he draws the blade of darkness, and shrouds them both in black.*

**KOTOS:**  
Andrella, hold — let us but speak a while.  
I'd have thee spare thy wrath, and hear me first.

*But the orb she keeps had shown her nothing kind — the very worst of futures, endless dark — and no charm nor cunning word was left in him to turn her.*

**ANDRELLA:**  
There is no speech that turns me from this hour.

*NORMAND, passing cards laid out upon a table, is drawn by the first row and draws the Empress.*

**NORMAND:** *[tucking it away]*  
For the Queen!

*He joins DEVERRE where he holds the berserkers back.*

**JACQUES:**  
How dost thou fare?

**NORMAND:**  
Oh, I'd not miss this brawl.

**JACQUES:** *[roaring]*  
For Rhidalia!

*The battle-cry rings out and steels each arm about them, a round's own strength lent sudden to their blows. JACQUES glances over his shoulder — the berserker in his grip yet squirms — and sees the LORD OF GLASS before the cards, reaching down to lift the World.*

**LORD OF GLASS:**  
Do not kill Qualton!

---

**SCENE VII. Andrella's chamber, and the tower's crown.**

**KOTOS:** *[quiet, in the dark]*  
Why dost thou seek to kill us, Andrella?

**ANDRELLA:** *[snarling]*  
Because thou meanst to strike the first blow, wizard.

*KOTOS senses through the dark that she is ragged, gaunt, and torn with grief. He leaps across her bed of tattered rags and snatches up the cracked crystal ball. In that instant a vision floods him — a Tattered Man, shrouded, watching from some other where — yet Kotos wrenches free his mind before the curse in it may take him whole.*

**KOTOS:**  
Farewell.

*He swallows a potion, turns to mist, and slips out through the gaps of stone and glass.*

**QUALTON:**  
Just so, my boy! *[his guardian-aura tears into the barbarians]*

**JACQUES:** *[gripping a barbarian fast]*  
What means he — "do not kill Qualton"?

**QUALTON:**  
Thou'rt doing  
Perfectly well. I'd love to meet this Lord  
Of Glass one day.

*KOTOS climbs again to the tower's very crown, and there, still gaseous, dances just beyond her reach — a lure, a bait, to draw ANDRELLA to the edge. She takes it: comes to the parapet, and flings a bolt of searing force at him, dramatic in her fury.*

**ANDRELLA:** *[at the parapet]*  
Then take this too!

*But the horn of Valhalla she keeps no longer for herself. Into the whipping dark she casts it — and a shape of shadow-wind, called by some the Black Wind, catches it from the air and bears it far away, beyond their reach forever.*

*Below, TOLMAR finds among the relics an amulet to bind and master the helmed horrors — yet tells the others nothing of it, and slips away with QUALTON, the prize kept close and secret in his hand.*

---

**SCENE VIII. The fall, and the hay below.**

*NORMAND seizes JACQUES, cries the battle-cry once more, and leaps with him out through the window; a ring of feather-lightness on his hand lets both come drifting gentle to the ground. ANDRELLA still struggles above.*

*KOTOS returns to the tower's crown, seizes ANDRELLA fast, and — rather than trust to chance — draws forth a small dark box, and by its strange art forces the very chance of things to choose the fall, abusing the unsteady nature of the tower itself. Together they plunge.*

*Below, the guardsmen — as a favor — had piled deep haystacks round the tower's base, and one fine heap lay waiting just below.*

*They land, and tumble free of one another. JACQUES tucks half of a torn card — the Fool — into ANDRELLA's pocket.*

**JACQUES:**  
Wilt thou consider now my suit, my lady?  
Wilt thou be wed to me? I never felt  
The feeling that I bear for thee, for any  
Soul I have met before in all my days.  
This will I swear before thy mother's eyes,  
If need there be for such a vow as this.

*ANDRELLA says nothing plain — the curse yet lies upon her, and no clear answer comes, only a long and searching silence.*

---

**SCENE IX. The claiming of the tower.**

*The tower stands now theirs, by right of blood and battle — yet TOLMAR keeps the cracked crystal for himself, and will not yield the amulet that masters the helmed horrors, however DEVERRE and KOTOS press him.*

*Among the last cards on the table, KOTOS draws the Tower, and is shown at once the worst of all the futures it may hold — an endless war of trees, root against root, world without end — and, dimly, what small thing might yet be done to turn that ending from its course.*

---

**SCENE X. The Baroness's charge.**

*The Baroness arrives, already bent upon their next design.*

**BARONESS:**  
Prepare thee now to take what thou dost need,  
And straightway ride for Garrot — there to learn  
What hand it was that struck my husband down.  
Its Viceroy, called Arna — or Arnes, or Arma —  
Rules there. Trust nothing that she offers thee.

*Then the Dark Knight lifts off his helm before them: an old man's face beneath, blue-eyed, long-bearded white as bone. He breathes upon them all, and on their mounts, and the Wind Walk takes them, turned to cloud and gale, and bears them through two hours' flight toward Garrot.*

**KOTOS:** *[to himself, aloft]*  
How do the Vaghalter play into this web?  
And Creyton — and the Tattered Man I saw —  
And what is meant by "Ahastyn," that word  
I cannot place, yet cannot put aside?  
Shall we ride into Garrot bold and loud,  
Or slip in shadow, since so long unseen  
They well may fail to know our faces there?

*Exeunt, on the wind.*
